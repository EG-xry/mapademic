About this map

What is this

8,396,458 OpenAlex authors with 15 or more works, positioned by a coauthorship ForceAtlas2 layout and colored by community. Hue encodes position, so neighboring fields share hues.

Built by Eric Gao, a student at UC San Diego.

Reading the map

Each dot is a researcher, placed near the people they write papers with and colored by their community: a field, or a national research network. Lighter and darker shades of a color are its subgroups, and bigger dots mean more citations. Top author dots mark the most-cited researchers before you zoom in close.

Faint lines join the million most frequent coauthor pairs. They are on at their faintest on computers (the gear's Line visibility slider brightens them); switch them on/off with Connection lines in the gear.

Cosmic dust (off by default; switch it on in the gear) is the faint grey haze of researchers the map cannot place in a field: authors with no coauthor links on the map, groups too small for the legend (under 1,000 researchers), and near empty records with no institution and few citations. Most of it forms the ring around the map's edge. These authors stay searchable either way.

Controls

  • Search finds one researcher and draws rays to their top coauthorships, colored by community membership - a ray's gradient means it crosses between fields. Rays work standalone, straight from OpenAlex, with no companion server required.
  • Connect finds the shortest coauthor path between two researchers, searched right in your browser over the map's whole coauthor network, colored the same way hop to hop, with the paper that links each step. Choose From: Institution to start from up to 50 most-cited researchers with an OpenAlex last-known affiliation. The result is the shortest among those mapped candidates, subject to the same search limits, not necessarily the whole institution.
  • Hover a dot at deep zoom to see the author's name and citations; click it for full details.
  • Share copies a link to the exact view.
  • The gear opens display settings, including connection lines between frequent coauthors, and an optional OpenAlex API key for live lookups.

Data

Built from the OpenAlex snapshot of 2026-09-23 (CC0): 8.4M authors and 366M coauthor links. Source code and method: mapademic on GitHub. Connect and the coauthor rays come from academic-degree-of-separation by riptideiv, whose backend powered them until October 2026; the search now runs in your browser.

Known issues in the data

OpenAlex builds its author profiles automatically, and they have errors that show up on the map:

  • Merged namesakes. Several people who share a common name can end up in one profile. Many of the biggest hubs are such merges, some listing hundreds of institutions in a single year. Their cards say so, and Connect routes around them when a path at most one step longer exists.
  • Split and duplicate profiles. One person can have several profiles: about one profile in 16 is a fragment of someone's record. Many Chinese, Japanese and Korean researchers also have a second profile in their own script for their domestic-language papers. It usually sits in its own, almost uncited cluster, away from their main profile.
  • Records that aren't people. A few thousand profiles are organizations, teams, typesetters or placeholders such as "unknown unknown".
  • Snapshot versus today. The map is the 2026-09-23 snapshot. OpenAlex keeps merging and splitting profiles, so a card's live details can differ from the profile's place on the map. Names come from OpenAlex live, or from the snapshot when OpenAlex can't answer.
  • Area names. Each colored area is named for the fields most typical of its coauthorship group. A small group folded into a larger area takes that area's name, so the label can differ from your own field; a card's Topics row shows the researcher's own.
